Understand AI risk

The Act looks at what an AI system is used for. Open an everyday example to see which rules can apply.

×Unacceptable riskAI infers how workers feel

Using AI to infer people’s emotions at work or in schools is generally prohibited. The exception is for systems intended for medical or safety reasons.

Read Article 5
!High riskAI screens job applicants

AI intended to filter applications or evaluate candidates can be high-risk. That can bring requirements for documentation, testing and human oversight. The intended use and classification rules matter.

Read Annex III Read Article 6
iTransparency riskA chatbot answers customers

Providers generally have to tell people when they are interacting directly with AI, unless it is obvious in context. Where notice is required, give it by the first interaction. Transparency duties can apply alongside other requirements.

Read Article 50
✓Minimal or no riskAn email filter catches spam

A typical spam filter is an example of minimal or no risk. It generally faces few AI Act-specific requirements, though other rules may still apply.

It depends on the use

Imagine a chatbot that answers store hours and screens job applications. People may need to be told they’re interacting with AI. The screening function can also be high-risk, so both sets of rules can apply.
