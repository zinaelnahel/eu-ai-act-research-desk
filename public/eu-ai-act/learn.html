Start with your situation

Find the rule, see which facts matter, and check the wording yourself.

My employer uses AI to monitor me. What should I look for?
The rule in plain language
AI designed to monitor workers’ performance or behaviour can be high-risk. Using AI to infer emotions at work is generally prohibited, with an exception for systems intended for medical or safety reasons.
What could change the answer?
Find out what the system measures and how the employer uses the result. Performance monitoring and emotion inference have different rules.
Annex III, point 4(b) Article 5(1)(f)
I’m building a chatbot. Do I have to tell users it’s AI?
The rule in plain language
Providers of AI that interacts directly with people generally have to tell them they’re interacting with AI. If notice is required, it should be clear and accessible by the first interaction.
What could change the answer?
Notice may be unnecessary if it’s already obvious in context. Article 50(1) also sets a limited exception for certain law-authorised criminal-offence systems.

Provider means the person or organisation that develops a system, or has it developed, and puts it on the market or into service under its own name or trademark.

Article 50(1) and (5)
AI helped make a decision about me. Can I ask for an explanation?
The rule in plain language
If Article 86 applies, you can ask the deployer for a clear explanation of the AI’s role and the main parts of the decision.
What could change the answer?
The decision must be based on an Annex III high-risk system, excluding point 2. It must have legal or similarly significant effects that you consider harmful to your health, safety or fundamental rights. Other applicable laws can affect this right.

Deployer means the person or organisation using the AI system under its authority, apart from personal, non-professional use.
